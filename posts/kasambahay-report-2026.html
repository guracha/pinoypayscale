2026 Kasambahay & Yaya Salary Guide (Philippines)

1. Executive Summary & Median Wage

The 2026 market analysis for Kasambahays (domestic helpers) and Yayas (nannies) in the Philippines indicates a median monthly wage of ₱8,000 for a standard live-in arrangement. This figure is influenced by the mandatory minimum wage set by the Domestic Workers Act (Batas Kasambahay), which varies by region. The market is characterized by high demand, informal employment practices, and increasing awareness of worker rights.

2. Kasambahay & Yaya Wage Benchmarks for 2026

Wages are primarily dictated by the government-mandated minimums, but often exceed these rates based on location, job scope (e.g., childcare vs. general housekeeping), and employment arrangement (live-in vs. live-out). Below is a breakdown of typical monthly wage ranges.

3. Key Market Dynamics & Trends

Chart: Wage growth in NCR vs. Provincial areas for live-in kasambahays.

Impact of Batas Kasambahay (Republic Act 10361)

The Domestic Workers Act has been pivotal in formalizing the sector. It mandates minimum wages, social security contributions, and proper employment contracts. While enforcement remains a challenge, it has empowered domestic workers and led to gradual improvements in working conditions and compensation.

Live-In vs. Live-Out Arrangements

There is a growing preference for live-out arrangements, especially in urban areas. Live-out helpers command a higher salary to cover transportation and living expenses. However, the live-in model remains dominant due to convenience for employers and the provision of accommodation for the worker, which is a significant non-cash benefit.

Role of Placement Agencies

Reputable placement agencies are becoming more common. They charge a one-time fee (often equivalent to one month's salary) for vetting, training, and placing workers. While this adds to the initial cost, it provides employers with a degree of security and a guarantee of replacement if the worker leaves prematurely. Agencies often command higher salaries for their sourced candidates.

4. Considerations for Employers

  • Adherence to Law is Non-Negotiable: Ensure you are compliant with the Batas Kasambahay, including wage, benefits (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG), and providing a weekly day off.
  • Clearly Define Job Scope: To avoid disputes, create a clear contract that outlines duties, work hours, and compensation. A Yaya focused solely on childcare should be compensated differently from an all-around Kasambahay.
  • Factor in Total Cost: The total cost of employment is not just the salary. It includes benefits, food, and utilities (for live-in staff). Budget accordingly to ensure you can sustainably meet your obligations.
