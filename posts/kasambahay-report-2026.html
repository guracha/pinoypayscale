2026 Philippine Kasambahay Salary & Labor Guide

A Comprehensive Guide to the Compensation, Rights, and Responsibilities of Domestic Workers in the Philippines

Updated: October 26, 2023

Mandatory Minimum Wage for 2026

Under the Batas Kasambahay (Republic Act No. 10361), domestic workers are entitled to a minimum wage. As of the latest wage order effective until mid-2026, the minimum monthly salaries are as follows:

Region Minimum Monthly Salary (PHP)
National Capital Region (NCR)₱6,500
Cities and 1st Class Municipalities₱5,500
Other Municipalities₱4,500

Important Note: These are the mandatory minimums. Employers and kasambahays are free to agree on a higher salary. The rates apply to all domestic workers, whether they are on a live-in or live-out basis.

Current Market Salary Rates in Key Urban Areas

While the law sets the floor, actual market rates in major cities are often higher due to demand. In high-end subdivisions and condominium communities, experienced kasambahays command more competitive salaries.

Mandatory Benefits Beyond Salary

The Batas Kasambahay ensures that domestic workers are not just paid a salary but are also covered by essential social security benefits. It is the employer's responsibility to register their kasambahay and remit contributions.

  • SSS (Social Security System): Provides retirement, disability, and other benefits.
  • PhilHealth: Ensures access to medical care and hospitalization services.
  • Pag-IBIG Fund: Offers housing loan programs and savings.

Employers are required to shoulder the full premium contribution if the kasambahay's monthly salary is less than ₱5,000. If it is ₱5,000 or more, the employee also contributes their share.

Rights and Privileges of a Kasambahay

  • Right to a written employment contract (Kontrata).
  • Right to a Certificate of Employment.
  • Right to 13th-month pay.
  • Right to at least 24 hours of consecutive rest per week.
  • Right to 5 days of annual service incentive leave with pay.
  • Right to be treated with dignity and respect.
  • Right to access education and training opportunities.
  • Right to terminate the employment relationship with just cause.
