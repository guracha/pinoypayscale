Housekeepers & Babysitters

Understanding the compensation landscape for "Ates" (Housekeepers) and "Yayas" (Babysitters) in the Philippines. Driven by inflation, specialized skills, and the Kasambahay Law.

📊 Executive Summary

The domestic labor market in 2026 has professionalized significantly. Employers are shifting from purely informal arrangements to structured compensation packages, heavily influenced by regional minimum wages and the demand for specialized childcare and culinary skills.

🍼 Babysitter (Yaya)

Yayas generally earn 20-30% more than general housekeepers due to the specialized nature of childcare, liability, and the required attention to detail.

Skill Premium Valuation

The Skill Premium

In 2026, finding a "generalist" is giving way to hiring specialists. Specific skills allow domestic helpers to negotiate significant premiums on top of their base salary.

  • 🗣️

    English Fluency (+15%)

    Highly sought after by expat families and households emphasizing early childhood language development.

  • 🍳

    Specialized Cooking (+20%)

    Ability to follow dietary restrictions (vegan, keto) or cook specific international cuisines.

  • ⚕️

    Infant Care & First Aid (+25%)

    Formal training in newborn handling, CPR, and pediatric first aid commands the highest premium.
